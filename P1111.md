## 0. Why now, and what "finished" means

The owner, 2026-11-10: the game is *"kind of going, kind of not finishing"*. About 70% of what
he wanted works, but *"it's not the thing"*, and it sits in his head and takes energy. He wants
it **finished in November 2026**, the way he sees it, and then to move on to other things.

The plan kept growing (Google day, the map maker, launch, selling), so the finish line kept
moving. **This file is the finish line.** Nothing is added to it during November. A new idea
goes to [§11](#11-what-waits).

**Finished** means the owner opens the game on his phone and on his laptop, and sees this:

> Four tabs at the bottom. In every one, the map of Uzbekistan is the main thing on the
> screen. He taps into Toshkent, picks Yunusobod, adds Buxoro, and plays there with friends.
> He opens "O‘rganish" and finds every region, every district, the mahallas of his own
> district, the world's countries with their flags and capitals, and the buses of Tashkent. He
> sends a quiz to a friend and they race. He plays a stranger one against one for rating. His
> own photo is on his profile. Nothing on any screen looks like a leftover.

Then, and only then, he buys the Yandex license and opens the game to everyone
([before-launch.md](before-launch.md), P1028 Stages 5 and 7).

## 1. The owner's intentions

Read [P1111-owner-words.md](P1111-owner-words.md) once, whole. In short:

- **Why the game exists.** He wanted to learn geography, found GeoGuessr, and wanted to play
  it *in his own country*. Google barely covers Uzbekistan; Yandex covers it widely. *"I knew
  I'm not alone … who doesn't know his country well enough, just lives, goes to work … But
  life is more than that. You should be curious about your country."*
- **For whom.** *"I want this game to be played by all Uzbeks, a lot of people, not just my
  friends."* Who they are: [players.md](players.md).
- **Show, don't type.** *"I'm a really visual person. I want to represent and show things."*
  Shapes on a map are remembered; lists of names are not. This is why the map of borders is
  the heart of every screen, and why the place-in-parent picture ([§5](#5-the-three-building-blocks))
  replaces banners and plain names.
- **Useful in life.** Knowing where Buxoro is, which district is which, where bus 67 goes.
  The world gets the basics; **Uzbekistan goes deep**: *"it's our nation, it's our place."*
- **Rivalry between people who know each other.** *"Humans like rivalry, competition. That's
  what grows people."* Friends first, strangers for rating second.
- **Taste.** Steve Jobs's way: the customer is the focus, **one thing at a time**, simple,
  intuitive. And GeoGuessr's finish: *"really good design, really good taste, really cool
  fonts, really gamified."* Ours must feel as finished, in our own way.
- **Honest about the code.** Much of today's code is, in his words, close to *"AI slop … not
  the exact thing what I want."* The fix is care: fewer things, each one right.

## 2. What the game becomes

**Assumed, the owner can say otherwise** (also in [§12](#12-for-the-owner)): playing alone
stays, on the O‘yin tab. The owner described that tab as "rating and random people" and did
not mention solo play, but it is the game's heart today and the only way to play when nobody
is online.

## 4. How each session works

Every prompt ends with "Follow P1111 §4". It means the eight steps below.

### Who does the work: one lead, many hands

The owner runs each session on **Fable**, and wants it used as the mind above the others:
*"Fable to be above it all, using sub-agents like Opus, Sonnet … a mastermind above all
models."* Tokens are not the limit; quality is: *"Make it perfect. Make it the way I want."*

- **The lead (Fable) thinks, decides and checks. It writes little code itself.** It reads the
  task and the owner's words, looks at what exists, decides the design, writes the contract
  between server and web (the OpenAPI file) and a brief for each helper, then judges what
  comes back by running it.
- **Opus agents build.** One slice each: the server side of a task, the web side, a hard bug,
  a piece of research that needs judgement. The taste review ([decision 0014](decisions/0014-taste-review.md))
  is an Opus agent that has not seen the work being made.
- **Sonnet agents do the plain work.** Data imports, translations into uz/ru/en, tests for
  finished code, screenshots, renames, searching the code.
- **In parallel when the slices don't touch.** Server and web can be built at the same time
  once the contract is fixed. Two agents never edit the same file.
- **Every brief carries the intention**, not only the job: who plays, what the owner said,
  what "done" looks like on a phone, what not to touch. A helper starts with nothing but its
  brief.
- **The lead trusts nothing it has not run.** A helper's "done" is a claim. The lead brings
  the game up and plays it before the task counts.

### The steps

1. **Branch `main`** in all three repos. "push" commits and pushes it ([access.md §2](access.md#2-git)).
2. **Read first**: the task's section here, the owner's words it points to, and
   `research/P1111-*.md` once session 1 has written them.
3. **Look before changing.** Play the screens the task touches as they are now. With
   `+w/b&as`, photograph them ([skills/before-after](skills/before-after/SKILL.md)).
4. **Build** through helpers, as above; `make test` (API), `npm run check` and `npm test`
   (web).
5. **docker up** and play it yourself ([local.md §3](local.md#3-docker-up)): phone size and
   laptop width, light and dark, on `demo` panoramas. The free Yandex key allows about 100
   panorama views a day; real Yandex rounds only when checking Yandex itself.
6. **The steve-jobs review** in a fresh agent: fix its musts, then review again
   ([skills/steve-jobs](skills/steve-jobs/SKILL.md)).
7. **Write it down**: tick the task here; update [roadmap.md](roadmap.md); update
   [design.md](design.md) when the look or a screen changed; a decision file when a
   reasonable person would have chosen differently ([decisions/](decisions/README.md)).
8. **Report** in [owner.md](owner.md#the-report)'s format. Nothing is committed until the
   owner says "push".

### Rules that hold in every task

- **One thing at a time.** One screen, one job, one main button.
- **Remove before adding.** When a new thing replaces an old one, the old one goes in the
  same task: its screen, its code, its API field, its text. No leftovers. This is the cure
  for what the owner calls slop.
- **If it's hard, say so and offer the closest thing** ([CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md#how-to-work-here)).
  Don't drop it, don't fake it.
- **Decent, clean, honest** ([players.md](players.md)): real numbers only, nothing bought
  that changes a score, no pressure to keep playing.
- **Uzbek first**, then Russian and English. `o‘` and `g‘` written properly everywhere.
- **Players see Yandex only** for panoramas and the playing map; our own map of borders for
  everything else ([decisions 0012](decisions/0012-yandex-map.md), [0015](decisions/0015-place-map.md)).

## 5. The three building blocks

Three things are used on almost every screen. They are described once, here.

### Place-in-parent

The owner's name for it, and his words: *"an SVG of the place with the parent … You may not
know the shape of Buxoro, but by looking at its parent and the selected part, you can
recognise it easily."*

A small picture: **the parent's whole shape, with the place inside it filled in the chosen
colour.**

| The place | The picture shows |
|---|---|
| A region (Toshkent viloyati, Buxoro viloyati, Qoraqalpog‘iston) | Uzbekistan, the region filled |
| Tashkent city | Toshkent viloyati, the city filled |
| A district of Tashkent (Yunusobod) | Tashkent city, the district filled |
| A district or city elsewhere (Jondor) | its region, the district filled |
| A mahalla | its district, the mahalla filled |
| Uzbekistan | Uzbekistan alone, filled |

It is the place's **face**, wherever a place is named: in the list of picked places, as the
back button of the map, on a round's result, in history, on a quiz question, on a profile's
"learned" list. It works at the size of an avatar (about 32 px) and as a large tile. The
other lines of the parent are drawn faintly, so the position reads at a glance.

### The map

Our own map of borders ([decision 0015](decisions/0015-place-map.md)), already built as the
place map of P1028 4.2. It becomes the main thing on the O‘yin, Do‘stlar and O‘rganish tabs.

- It opens on **Uzbekistan**. No back button there.
- **A tap goes in**: a region shows its districts and cities; Tashkent shows its districts.
- Inside a place, the **top-left corner holds a small square with that place's
  place-in-parent**. It tells the player where they are, and **a tap on it goes up one
  level**. Inside Tashkent it shows Tashkent in its region; one tap shows the region; there
  it shows the region in Uzbekistan; one more tap shows Uzbekistan.
- Inside a place there is one button, **"Tanlash"**, that picks it.

### The picked places

A game can be played across **several places of any level at once**: Yunusobod, Buxoro and
Samarqand together, *"as many places, as deep, as high as we want."*

- Each picked place appears in a **list**, as its place-in-parent and its name, with a way to
  take it out. On a laptop the list is at the left and the map takes the rest. On a phone
  the map stays the main thing and the list is a row of the same small pictures.
- **Picked places are marked on the map** in their own colour, at every level: on the map of
  Uzbekistan, a region that holds a picked district shows it.
- Picking a place that contains places already picked replaces them with it.

## 6. Stage 0: look before you build

One session, no changes players see. Findings go to `research/` in this knowledge base, one
file each, short, with sources and a verdict.

### ☐ 0.1 The models, played

Play them, don't only read about them. Write `research/P1111-references.md`.

- **StudyGe** (`studyge.com`, the owner plays it): how a question is asked, how the map
  answers a right and a wrong tap, how small countries are tapped, its learn mode against
  its test mode, how levels grow. The owner: *"really clever, really visual, intuitive."*
- **Squiz** (`squiz-app.com`, on Google Play as `com.squiz.app`): the quiz with choices, how
  it repeats what was missed. The owner: *"making it a bit easier … in some cases we can use
  it."*
- **GeoGuessr**: the look and the lettering; the moment a player scores 5 000; duels (health,
  how damage grows by round, the timer after the first guess); the rating and its divisions.
- **For each: what we take, what we leave, and why.** We take ideas and feelings, never
  pictures, sounds, texts or licensed fonts. The game stays *"our own way"* ([CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md#what-the-owner-cares-about)).

### ☐ 0.2 Data: Tashkent's mahallas

`research/P1111-mahallas.md`. Is there a full, correct, usable set of mahalla borders for
Tashkent city? Check OpenStreetMap first (the game's places already come from it), then any
open official source. For each: how many of the city's mahallas, with borders that meet, with
names in Uzbek; the licence. **Verdict:** all of Tashkent, or the districts that are complete,
or not possible now and the closest thing (for example mahallas as points to find). The owner
agreed to **Tashkent only**; the rest of the country waits ([§11](#11-what-waits)).

### ☐ 0.3 Data: Tashkent's buses

`research/P1111-buses.md`. Are the routes in OpenStreetMap complete and current enough to
draw? Compare a few with Yandex's or the city's own listing. Then **propose the list**: the
owner wants *"very, very popular buses … where they go really popular places"*, at least 10,
30 if the data allows. There is likely no open count of passengers, so say how the list was
chosen (long routes through the centre, the big bazaars, stations and universities) and let
the owner strike and add. **Verdict and the list.**

### ☐ 0.4 Data: the world; and the knowledge base

- `research/P1111-world.md`: country shapes that are free to use and light enough for a
  phone; flags as free SVGs; capitals; every name in Uzbek, Russian and English. Use one
  neutral, standard list of countries (the UN's members) so the game takes no side in any
  border dispute, and note where the shapes source and that list disagree.
- **Bring the knowledge base up to date** with this file: [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md) ("Why it
  exists" now holds the wider intention of [§1](#1-the-owners-intentions); "Where things
  are"), [README.md](README.md), [roadmap.md](roadmap.md), the decisions' index (0022), and a
  note at the top of [P1028.md](P1028.md) that P1111 comes first.

**Done when** the owner can read four short findings and knows what Stage 3 will really be.

## 7. Stage 1: the foundation

### ☐ 1.1 The new look

The owner: GeoGuessr has *"really good design, really good taste … really cool fonts, really
gamified, truly cool. We need to have such things too. We have some lacks about these fonts."*
Asked whether to keep today's road-sign style and change only the fonts, or change the whole
look, he chose **the whole look**.

- **A game, not a form**: bold display lettering, strong colour, depth, buttons that feel
  pressed, motion with a purpose. As finished as GeoGuessr, and not a copy of it: its fonts
  are its own, so ours are free ones with the same confidence, that carry Uzbek Latin (`o‘`,
  `g‘`), Cyrillic and digits well.
- **The map of Uzbekistan is the identity.** The shapes, the place-in-parent pictures and
  the colours of "picked", "learned" and "missed" are what a player remembers.
- **First on three screens only**: the home, a round, a result. The session stops there and
  shows the owner, with before and after. This is the one place in the plan where the owner's
  yes is needed before going on, because every later screen is drawn with it.
- After the yes (session 3): every screen, light and dark, phone and laptop;
  [design.md](design.md) rewritten for the new look (its "Signs, not cards" goes); a decision
  file. The design skills in [skills/](skills/) are for this.

**Done when** the owner says the three screens are the look, and then no screen is left in
the old one.

### ☐ 1.2 Place-in-parent, everywhere

Build the picture of [§5](#place-in-parent) as one component, fed by the server's shapes
(`/v1/places/{slug}/shape` exists; give it what it lacks, such as the place with its parent
in one answer, cached).

- **Banners go.** The owner: the banner *"actually sucks … I didn't want a banner, I wanted
  it as an avatar showing place in parent … I randomly said banner, and that's what Claude
  Code implemented."* Remove the banner from the game and from the API (`banner`,
  `bannerCredit`), with its photos and credits.
- Put the picture **wherever a place is named**: a round's result, the end of a game,
  history, the room's line of places, the profile's learned places.

**Done when** no banner is left, and a player who has never seen Jondor's shape can tell
where it is from its picture alone.

### ☐ 1.3 The map picker; games across several places

The map and the picked places of [§5](#the-map), built from today's place map
(`PlaceMap.svelte`, P1028 4.2), keeping what [decision 0016](decisions/0016-place-map-whole.md)
settled (whole places; a place with few panoramas is named in italics).

Server: a game, a room and a challenge can hold **a list of places**.

- **A round first picks one of the places, each equally likely, then a panorama inside it.**
  Buxoro is not drowned by Tashkent's far larger pool.
- **A round is scored by the size of its own place**, not of everything picked. Otherwise,
  with Yunusobod and Buxoro picked together, any guess inside Tashkent would score almost
  5 000. The guess map opens showing all the picked places.
- The ready-made maps ("Toshkent ko‘chalari") stay pickable beside places.
- No street twice in one game still holds.

**Done when** the owner picks Yunusobod, Buxoro and Samarqand in a few taps, sees all three
marked on the map and listed with their pictures, and plays rounds from each.

### ☐ 1.4 The four tabs

The bar of [§2](#2-what-the-game-becomes), at the bottom on a phone, at the side or top on a
laptop. It is hidden during a round. The home with one Play button (P1028 2.1) is replaced by
the O‘yin tab.

- **O‘yin**: the map, the place picked, one big "O‘ynash" for playing alone. The one-against-one
  buttons arrive in Stage 5; until then the tab is complete without them.
- **Do‘stlar**: task 2.1.
- **O‘rganish**: until Stage 3 it holds what exists and belongs there: "Adashgan ko‘chalarim"
  and the streets learned.
- **Profil**: until Stage 4, today's profile, settings and shop, moved under it.
- "Kodim bor" (a friend's code) lives on Do‘stlar. Challenge links keep working.

**Done when** every screen of the game is reached from one of the four tabs in at most two
taps, and nothing is reachable only through an old menu.

## 8. Stage 2: rooms and the small fixes

### ☐ 2.1 The Do‘stlar tab

The owner: *"you will create a room, you will see your connected people, friends, and you
can play together … the main thing is fun with friends."* And: *"in the rooms tab I see how
many friends we have on the top, because the main thing is given to the map."*

- The tab **is** the room's lobby: the map as the main thing, the friends' faces across the
  top, the picked places, invite (as built in P1028 2.2), "Boshlash".
- Everyone plays for themselves. **Teams wait** ([§11](#11-what-waits)).
- The host picks the places on the map; the others see them appear.

### ☐ 2.2 The compass

- **A tap on the compass turns the view to north.** The owner: it changes the direction
  *"horizontally, looking directly to north. It doesn't change it vertically."* The tilt up
  or down stays as it was. With "move" and "stay"; not in the fully still mode, where the
  view cannot turn.
- **The compass's style leaves the room's settings.** *"Everyone will select their compasses
  in their profile."* Whether a game has a compass at all stays a rule of the game.

### ☐ 2.3 Guests can rename themselves, any time

The owner: guests get in, and then *"couldn't change their names"* until the game ends or
they leave. A guest can change their name **in the lobby, during a game and at its end**. If
changing it in the middle of a round is awkward, offer it right after each guess. Everyone in
the room sees the new name at once; the name filter still applies. Only guests need this; a
signed-in player has their profile.

### ☐ 2.4 The 5 000 moment

*"In GeoGuessr … it has a really cool animation, and I think we need to have such animation
too, for 5K."* A perfect round gets its own celebration: seen, heard and felt, short, and
never in the way of the next round. Ours, not a copy. In a room, everyone sees whose it was.

**Done when** the owner plays a room with a guest on a second phone and meets all four.

## 9. Stage 3: O‘rganish

The owner: *"this app should help others to learn about geography as well … the visualisation,
where it's located and the shape of it."* None of this uses Yandex, so it has no daily limit
and costs nothing to run.

### ☐ 3.1 The quiz engine

One engine for every quiz below, so they all feel the same.

- **Kinds of question:** *find it on the map* (a name is given, tap its shape: StudyGe's
  way, the main kind); *name it* (a shape is lit, pick its name from a few: Squiz's way);
  and, for the world, a flag or a capital in place of the name.
- **Two ways to play each quiz:** *O‘rganish* (no clock; a wrong tap shows the right answer;
  what was missed comes back until it is known) and *Sinov* (a test: points, time, a result).
- **Levels** that grow: a few easy ones first, then all of them.
- **Progress is kept with the account**, and shown on the map: what a player knows is
  coloured in.
- Quizzes earn no Barg for now ([§11](#11-what-waits)).

### ☐ 3.2 Uzbekistan: regions and districts

- **Regions:** the name is given, tap it on the map of Uzbekistan.
- **Districts of one region or of Tashkent:** *"I want to learn every district in Tashkent."*
- **Every district of the country, in two steps:** *"We'll give just the name of the
  district, like Yunusobod … they will click on Tashkent and then click on the district. And
  maybe it's Jondor: they go to Buxoro and select it there."* Knowing the region is half the
  answer.

### ☐ 3.3 Tashkent's mahallas

*"One step deeper … Mahalla, kvartal … it makes it truly high level."* Inside a district of
Tashkent, find its mahallas, drawn the same way as everything above. What exactly is built
follows the verdict of 0.2.

### ☐ 3.4 The world

*"About the world, we don't have to go that deep. Basic things."* Countries on the map, by
continent and then the whole world; flags; capitals. Small countries must be easy to tap on a
phone (0.1 shows how StudyGe does it). Names in Uzbek first.

### ☐ 3.5 Tashkent's buses

*"There will be shown the route of the bus … and they should identify which bus it is. Is it
115 or is it 14? … It will be useful for a lot of people."* A route is drawn on the map of
Tashkent; pick its number. After the answer, the route's main stops are named, because that
is the useful part. The buses are the owner's approved list from 0.3: *"not every bus, but
the popular, needed ones."*

### ☐ 3.6 Any quiz, with friends

*"These games also could be played with friends … how good are they … live, one versus
one."* And how: *"not adding this in the room tab, but an option in the learning part … by
clicking on it, they will go to the room tab, and everything is set."*

- On every quiz, a smaller button: **"Do‘stlar bilan"**. It opens a room on the Do‘stlar tab
  with that quiz already chosen; the host may change its settings; friends join as they join
  any room.
- Two players or more. Everyone gets the same questions. **Points for right answers, and the
  time is counted.** The server judges, as it does for streets. The end is the room's podium.

**Done when** the owner learns the districts of Tashkent until the map is fully coloured,
then beats a friend at flags from a link.

## 10. Stages 4, 5 and 6

### ☐ 4.1 The Profil tab

One place for: the photo or emoji and the name; the rating (once Stage 5 exists); what the
player has learned, shown on the map; Barg and the shop, as they are
([decision 0021](decisions/0021-barg.md)); the compass's style and the other settings; the
ways to sign in.

### ☐ 4.2 Your own photo

The owner: emojis were only ever meant for those without a photo, *"something easily
recognisable, distinguishable."* GeoGuessr gives avatars many ways to be changed; we don't,
*"so we should give them the option to upload a photo."*

- A signed-in player uploads a photo, crops it to a square, and it is their face everywhere
  an emoji was: rooms, podiums, tables, their page. A shop frame goes round it.
- No photo: the emoji, as today. Guests keep emojis.
- **Where it is kept:** made small on the server and stored in Postgres, which is already
  backed up; no new service. If the game later needs file storage for something else, it
  moves to MinIO then.
- The report button that exists for names covers photos, and `shafaq-tools` can take one
  down ([decision 0013](decisions/0013-accounts.md)).

### ☐ 5.1 The duel

One against one, **GeoGuessr's way, which the owner prefers to a fixed number of rounds:**
*"you play until the opponent's health is gone."*

- Both start with the same health. After each round, whoever scored less loses the
  difference; later rounds hurt more. Once one player has guessed, the other has a short
  time left. The exact numbers come from 0.1 and from playing it.
- Built first as a kind of room, so two friends can duel from the Do‘stlar tab. That is also
  how the owner tests it before strangers exist.

### ☐ 5.2 Strangers

On the O‘yin tab:

- **For rating:** the whole of Uzbekistan, always. *"You can only play in Uzbekistan because
  it's about rating."* Signed-in players only.
- **Just for fun:** pick one place, *"like Yunusobod"*, and meet someone who picked the same.
  No rating.
- **Nobody there?** Say so plainly and let the player decide. The owner: *"just let him know
  about the current situation, that's it."* No invented opponents, no fake counts
  ([players.md](players.md)).

### ☐ 5.3 The rating

An Elo-style number that moves after each rated duel, shown on the profile, with a table of
the best players. Kept simple: one number, one table.

### ☐ 5.4 Fair, and closed until the license

- **Cheating, said honestly:** Yandex's viewer must be given the place, so a determined
  cheater can read it. What we do: rating for accounts only, the report button, and the
  server noticing guesses that are too good too fast. It is not cheat-proof, and the game
  does not claim to be.
- **The free key cannot carry strangers.** About 100 panorama views a day would be gone in
  minutes. So the stranger buttons sit behind one server setting, off on the live server
  until the Yandex license is bought ([before-launch.md](before-launch.md)), on locally for
  the owner to play.
