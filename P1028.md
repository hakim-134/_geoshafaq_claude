## 0. The goal, and what "finished" means

The owner's words: *"Making a platform for Uzbekistan where people can play in our home
country, and learn things they need in life: helpful, useful, practical."*

**Finished** means a stranger gets through this without anyone's help:

> A friend in Tashkent opens the game from a link in a Telegram group. One tap later they
> are standing on a street. They guess, see where they really were, learn the street's name
> and the nearest metro, and play again. They invite two friends in two taps; the three of
> them play a room that ends on a podium. Tomorrow they come back for the daily challenge, and
> their map of Tashkent has one more district coloured in.

Every task below answers three questions:

1. **Is it simpler?** One screen, one job, one big button.
2. **Does it teach something useful?** A street, a district, a way to get there.
3. **Is it better with friends?** Inviting, playing together, comparing.

## 3. Stage 1: taste, the Steve Jobs review

The owner: *"We need a skill of Steve Jobs, so it can check the product from the browser and
give feedback to the frontend agents."* Why it is missing: a session that builds a screen judges
it by the code it wrote. Nobody looks at the product the way a player does, with fresh eyes and
no patience.

### ☐ 1.1 The skill and the playtest script

`_geoshafaq_claude/skills/steve-jobs/SKILL.md` (it loads through `.claude/skills`, like the
other skills). It reviews; it never writes code.

**Who it is.** A product reviewer who works the way Steve Jobs was known to: from the person
holding the phone backwards, never from the code forwards. Two of his lines set the tone:
"focus is about saying no" (WWDC, 1997) and "Design is how it works" (*The New York Times
Magazine*, 2003).

**What it believes, for this game:**

1. **One screen, one job.** Each screen has one thing to do, and it is the biggest thing on it.
   Two big buttons: one goes.
2. **Saying no.** The best fix is usually a removal. Cut before adding; merge what is there
   twice ("Toshkent" and "Toshkent ko‘chalari").
3. **No manual.** A screen that needs a sentence on how to use it ("three steps to open a
   room") is wrong; the player isn't.
4. **The owner's test: play it as a 5-year-old would.** Someone who reads badly and has never
   seen the game knows what to do in three seconds. Pictures, colour and motion before words.
5. **Fast to the street.** From opening the site to standing on a street: one tap, a few
   seconds. Every extra tap has to earn its place.
6. **Numbers tell the truth.** Every number explains itself where it shows (a hint's −20%).
7. **No surprises.** A tap does what it looks like. Nothing throws the player out of the game
   by accident.
8. **The parts nobody notices are the product too:** spacing, alignment, a misspelled street,
   a blurry icon, half a laptop screen left empty.
9. **Delight in the right places.** The reveal, a perfect round, the podium get sound and
   motion; everything else stays calm.
10. **True to the players:** decent and honest ([players.md](players.md)), Uzbek first, made
    for friends.

**The bar here:** the home has one green button; a link to a street is at most two taps;
inviting friends is two taps; no sentence on a game screen longer than about eight words;
everything works one-handed on a phone, in light and dark.

**How it reviews:**

1. **Look first, read later.** Open the game before reading code, plans or design.md, and
   write first impressions. Then read [design.md](design.md) and [players.md](players.md) for
   the brand and the decency rules.
2. A headless Chrome with its own throwaway profile, demo panoramas on (never in the owner's
   Chrome: [local.md §1](local.md#1-what-runs)). Phone 390×844 light, then dark, then a
   1366×800 laptop.
3. Walk what a stranger walks: first visit → first street → result → end of the game; a
   friend's room link → join → play → podium; hosting a room; choosing another place; coming
   back the next day; sharing a result.
4. On every screen: a screenshot, one sentence on what a stranger sees, the one thing to do,
   how many choices there are, and every element marked keep / cut / merge / fix.
5. Count: taps and seconds from opening the site to a street; taps to invite a friend; words
   on the screen.

**What it hands over.** A short review, worst first, ten points at most. Each point names the
screen, says what is wrong in a player's words, gives the exact change for the frontend session
("remove the three lower O‘ynash buttons; the sign above the one button opens the place map"),
and says how to tell it's fixed. The top three are marked **must**. It ends with **Ship it** or
**Not yet**. Fixes use the other skills ([game-ui-frontend](skills/game-ui-frontend/SKILL.md)
first).

**Where it sits.** At the end of every task that changes what players see: build → docker up →
the review in a fresh agent (fresh eyes: it doesn't know what the builder meant) → fix the
musts → review again → report to the owner. The owner can also say **"steve jobs"** at any time
for a review of what is running now.

**The playtest script**, same task: `geo-shafaq/scripts/playtest/`, a small puppeteer-core
script (`npm run playtest`) that plays the journeys on demo panoramas (a solo game with a hint,
a two-player room to its end, the place picker) and saves screenshots for phone light, phone
dark and laptop. Then a review costs minutes, not a session. It runs Chrome with
`--disable-gpu` ([local.md §3](local.md#3-docker-up)) and its own temporary profile, so the demo
switch never reaches the owner's browser. Yandex's own site answers headless browsers with
"limited" (429): check links to Yandex Maps in a normal browser.

**Done when:** the skill reviews the game as it is today and its top three match §1.

### ☐ 2.2 Rooms that explain themselves

The owner: *"The rooms part really needs to be intuitive, and easy to use and understand."*

**(a) One tap to invite**

- "Do‘stlar bilan o‘ynash" opens a room at once, with the home's place and settings. No map to
  pick first.
- The lobby has one job, bringing friends in: one big "Do‘stlarni chaqirish" button (the
  phone's share sheet; Telegram is the first choice on most Uzbek phones), the number plate
  smaller under it for reading aloud, a QR icon for friends in the same room, players popping
  in with a sound as they join, one line for the place and rules (the host taps it to change
  them), and "Boshlash · 3" at the bottom. The explaining sentences go: the buttons say it.
- If the phone allows a share right after the tap, the share sheet opens by itself, and the
  invite takes two taps. Phones only allow it soon after a tap, and only over https; otherwise
  the big button is the first thing under the thumb. On a laptop the link is copied ("Nusxa
  olindi") and the QR code shows.
- Joining by link stays as it is: no questions. "Kodim bor" on the home opens the number plate
  to type a code on; it joins by itself at five letters, as now.

**(b) The end is a stage**

- A podium: third, second and first rise one by one, the winner's confetti on every phone, and
  each phone says its own place ("Siz 2-o‘rindasiz").
- One next step: the host's "Yana o‘ynash" (everyone stays, new streets); friends see "Xona
  egasi yangi o‘yin boshlaydi" and are taken in by themselves. "Natijani ulashish" is the one
  quiet second button; settings, my game and leaving go under ⋯.
- The end map shows every round's badge (3.1, tappable: 3.2) and the player's own pins.

**(c) The next round starts by itself** (recommended)

- After a round's result, a ten-second ring counts down on every phone, then the next round
  starts. The host can press "Hozir" or hold it. Nobody waits on one person's tap.
- A server change: the room's result phase gets a deadline like a round's, kept in the Valkey
  snapshot (`api-geo-shafaq/internal/room`).

- **How:** `geo-shafaq/src/routes/room/[code]/+page.svelte`, `src/lib/room.svelte.ts`, the
  home (2.1); the server for (c).
- **Done when:** from the home a host sends the invite in two or three taps; a friend joins from
  the link with none; three phones play to the podium; the host's one tap starts the next game
  for everyone.

_Read as written: "In Room end of game is kinda stage" taken as "kinda strange". Either way the
end becomes a stage._

### ☐ 2.3 The hint's cost, shown

The owner: *"You can see that the player is closer but has fewer points."*

- In the result panel, when hints cost points:

  ```text
  4 412  −20% 💡      small, grey: the score before the hint, and its cost
  3 530               big: what the player got
  ```

  The score counts up to 4 412, the 💡 chip drops in, and the number ticks down to 3 530 with a
  soft note: the player sees the cause. Two hints show "−20% −20%" (they multiply: 64% is
  kept, as `score.WithHints` does).
- In a room's round table: a 💡 on the row of everyone who took a hint, with their score before
  it in grey. On the final table, the number of hints next to the name. The game page and
  history show the same.
- Free hints ("Oson") show nothing: there is no cost to explain.
- **How:** the server sends the score before hints (`base`) with every result: `RoundResult`
  and `RoomScore` in `api/openapi.yaml`, filled in `internal/game` and `internal/room`. The
  formula stays only in `internal/score`. The web shows it: `RoundReveal.svelte`,
  `ScoreCount.svelte`, the room's table.
- **Done when:** in a room on "O‘rta", the friend who took a hint sees why the closer guess got
  fewer points, and so does everyone else.

## 5. Stage 3: the result map

### ☐ 3.1 The real place's marker

The owner: *"We need an outstanding looking icon for the actual location 🏁, like in the
original GeoGuessr: their icon looks pretty good."*

What makes GeoGuessr's markers look good, without copying their drawing: they are compact round
badges with a white ring and a real shadow, they read at a glance on any map, and the real place
never looks like a guess. Ours:

- **A round badge, about 36 px:** a white ring, a green disc (green stays the real place's
  colour), and in it a white finish flag 🏁 with a small chequer. A short point under the badge
  marks the exact spot, with a soft shadow on the ground.
- It drops in with the bounce and the ring it has now.
- **On the end map** each badge carries its round's number in a small gold dot on its shoulder,
  so the badges don't stand on each other's labels; where two overlap, the later round sits on
  top and both numbers stay visible.
- Players' pins stay drops in their own colours. Nothing else on the map is green with a white
  ring.
- Checked on all three map looks (Xarita, Sputnik, Gibrid: green on green fields is why the
  white ring is there), light and dark, on ordinary and sharp screens.
- The same badge in the legend, on the game page and in rooms.
- **How:** `flagSvg`, `flagMarker`, `dropMarker` in `geo-shafaq/src/lib/map/map.ts`; `.sg-drop`
  in `app.css`; the legend in `ResultLayout.svelte`; design.md's line on the real place.
- **Done when:** on the end map of a five-round game every badge is clear at a glance, and none
  hides another or the city's names.

### ☐ 3.2 Tap the flag: the real panorama on Yandex Maps

The owner: *"Make it clickable like in GeoGuessr: when clicked, open a new tab in Yandex Maps
with that place's panorama, so players can check and work on them."*

- Tapping a real place's badge opens a new tab with that place's panorama on Yandex Maps,
  facing the way the round started:

  ```text
  https://yandex.uz/maps/?panorama[point]=<lon>,<lat>&panorama[direction]=<heading>,0&panorama[span]=120,60
  ```

  Checked on 2026-10-28 in a real Chrome: the panorama opens full screen (Yandex adds
  `panorama[full]=true`), in Uzbek, on the nearest panorama (16 m off at Amir Temur square).
  Longitude comes first.
- **Where:** every round's result (one badge), the end of a game (every badge), a room's round
  result and end, the game page (`/game/<id>`), and each street in "my streets".
- The street plate's "Xaritada ochish" opens the same panorama: one place to go, not two.
- On a laptop the badge says "Panoramada ko‘rish ↗" on hover; on a phone, the first result
  shows a short tip once: "Bayroqni bosing: shu joyni Yandex panoramasida ko‘ring".
- Only after the result: during a round nothing links to the place.
- Inside Telegram (its browser, later the Mini App) the link opens through Telegram's own
  `openLink`, so the game stays open. On a phone with the Yandex Maps app the link may open the
  app; check on the owner's phone.
- **How:** the badge's placemark becomes clickable (today `interactivityModel:
  'default#transparent'`); the result's pairs in `ResultMap.svelte` carry the round's heading
  (`rounds.heading` on the server, sent with the result); `mapUrl` in `StreetPlate.svelte`. The
  URL is built in one function in `map.ts`, not spread around.
- **Done when:** on a phone, tapping each badge of a finished game opens that street's panorama
  on Yandex Maps, facing the way the round began.

### ☐ 3.3 The crowd circle

The owner: *"At the end of one round, not of all rounds: add a pulsing radius around the actual
point, the average of all players' guesses."*

- On every round's result, a soft gold ring around the real place. Its radius is the
  **average distance** of everyone's guesses that round. It pulses out from the badge twice
  when the badge lands, then rests, with a small label on its edge: "O‘rtacha 2.3 km".
- One line in the panel says what it means for this player: "O‘rtachadan 1.2 km yaqinroq"
  (closer than the average), or "uzoqroq" (farther).
- **In a room:** the room's players that round, two guesses or more. No server change: the
  result already has everyone's distance.
- **Alone:** everyone who ever played that panorama, from the server, shown only when at least
  three other players guessed it. Few panoramas have that today, so alone it shows mostly in
  challenges and the daily (Stage 6), where everyone plays the same streets.
- Gold: not green (the real place), not red (the line), not a player's colour.
- The map frames the ring when it isn't far bigger than the guess; otherwise the ring runs past
  the edges, which says the same.
- **How:** the ring is a `ymaps.Circle` (in metres, like the hint circle); the pulse is a
  placemark with an HTML layout sized from the zoom, a CSS animation, redrawn on zoom, and still
  for reduced motion. Alone: the API adds `crowd: {km, n}` to `RoundResult`, the average of
  `guesses.km` over rounds with the same `panorama_id`, other players only, guessed and not
  late.
- **Done when:** in a three-player room every round's result shows the ring at the average
  distance, and each player's line says closer or farther.

### ☐ 4.1 Server: a place's map

- `GET /v1/places/{slug}/map`: the place's children as SVG paths in one shared frame, each with
  its slug, name (uz / ru / en), kind, a point for its label, its panorama count and the
  player's progress, plus the parent's outline.
- **Neighbours share one border:** the children are simplified together as a coverage
  (`ST_CoverageSimplify`; `ST_CoverageClean` first if OpenStreetMap's borders don't quite fit),
  projected (`ST_Transform` to 3857), scaled into the frame and written with `ST_AsSVG`. Label
  points from `ST_MaximumInscribedCircle`. All of these are in the local PostGIS 3.6.4 (GEOS
  3.14) and in the server's PostGIS 3.6 image.
- One level is tens of kilobytes, cached until the places change.
- **How:** `api-geo-shafaq/internal/places`, `api/openapi.yaml`, then `make gen` and
  `npm run gen`.

### ☐ 4.2 The place map

- `/places` opens on the map of Uzbekistan: the 14 parts as a flat, warm picture (paper fill,
  lapis borders, the girih texture faint), names on them.
- **Tashkent stands out.** On the country map the city is a few pixels wide, so it gets a big
  round badge; on a first visit it glows, with "Shu yerdan boshlang".
- Tap a region: the map flies into it (a CSS transform, about 400 ms) and its districts and
  cities appear, with a back arrow and the place's name on top. Tap Tashkent: its 13 tumanlar.
- Tap a district: it lights up, and a card rises with its entry sign, its panoramas, the
  player's progress, and one button, "O‘ynash".
- Places without panoramas are hatched, say "Tez orada", and can't be played.
- **Colour is what the player knows:** paper for unplayed, light gold for seen, deeper gold as
  streets are learned (the progress colours the picker uses now).
- Under the map: the official maps as signs (Toshkent ko‘chalari, Tarixiy shaharlar, Butun
  O‘zbekiston), and the places as a list for those who prefer one (and for keyboards and
  screen readers).
- Phone first: at each level the shapes are big enough to tap; no pinch-zoom needed.
- **How:** a `PlaceMap.svelte` in `geo-shafaq/src/lib/maps/`, used by `src/routes/places/`.
- **Done when:** from the home's sign a player goes Uzbekistan → Tashkent → Chilonzor and starts
  a game there in three taps, on a phone, light and dark.

### ☐ 4.3 My map

- The profile's big picture: Tashkent's 13 tumanlar coloured by how well the player knows them,
  with the streets learned. A tap on a district shows its streets ("my streets").
- On a laptop it fills the home's right half (2.1).
- The learning goal made visible: colouring in your own city.
- **Done when:** after a few games a player sees which parts of Tashkent they know and which
  they don't.

## 8. Stage 6: together, every day (PLAN Phase 8)

## 10. Stage 8: Barg (PLAN Phase 9)

Phase 9 splits in two, because only selling needs a company.

rooms and on leaderboards.

## 11. Useful and practical: where it goes next

The main goal asks for more than streets. Ideas that fit it, for after Stage 6, best value first:

1. **One more useful fact each round.** The plate already names the street, district and
   nearest metro (the server keeps 49 stations). Add one landmark within a few hundred metres,
   from OpenStreetMap: a bozor, a park, a hospital, a university, a public service centre.
2. **Practical maps**, official ones made with the map maker (8.1): "Metro bekatlari" (you
   stand at an entrance: which station?), "Bozorlar", "Davlat xizmatlari markazlari",
   "Shifoxonalar", "Oliygohlar". The location pipeline (PLAN §4) picks panoramas near such
   points.
3. **Walk on from the real place:** 3.2's link already takes players to the street on Yandex.
4. **Missed streets come back** in later games until they are learned (beyond 8.5).

Each one passes the three questions of §0. Each needs the location pipeline to fill the country
beyond v0's pools: 3 036 panoramas today, 2 507 of them in Tashkent.
